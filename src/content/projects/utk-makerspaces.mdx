## The problem

Public high schools in Peru and Colombia had students eager to build things and no labs, tools or trained teachers to build them with. Donated equipment tended to sit in boxes because nobody had a program around it.

## What we built

**United Technologies for Kids (UTK)** treated the makerspace as a product, not a donation: the lab, a curriculum, and trained instructors to run it.

- Designed the program model: equipment kit, curriculum, and an instructor pipeline.
- Built a network of **150 undergraduate instructors** by partnering with MIT International Science and Technology Initiatives (MISTI), the MIT J-WEL lab, and INvent student organizations at UC Berkeley and Stanford.
- Rolled out STEM labs and programs in **more than 150 schools** across Peru and Colombia.

## Product lessons

- **The hardware was the easy part.** Adoption depended on people: a lab with a trained instructor got used, and a lab without one didn't.
- **Scale came from a repeatable playbook,** not from heroics. Each new university chapter followed the same training and deployment steps.
